The world spends roughly $115 trillion a year. Where that money flows decides what gets built, what gets burned, and what survives. This page follows the biggest flows — the unconscious ones quietly financing the damage, and the conscious ones already starting to reverse it.

START HERE

The premise of this site: climate change and nature loss are usually described as energy problems or land problems. Underneath, they are capital allocation problems. Coal plants, cattle pastures and container ships all exist because a stream of money keeps them profitable — and solar farms, restored wetlands and green steel exist exactly where a stream of money was consciously pointed at them.

"Unconscious" spending doesn't mean malicious. It means money moving by default — subsidies nobody remembers enacting, damage no invoice ever prices in, purchases nobody connects to their consequences. The largest numbers on this page are not evil schemes; they are autopilot.

The good news is that redirecting money is far easier than inventing it. The sums needed to fund the entire solutions portfolio are small next to the flows already sloshing through the system — which is what makes conscious cash a genuine lever, not a slogan.

UNCONSCIOUS SPENDING

The two biggest bars above deserve unpacking, because almost nobody voted for them.

The $7.4 trillion fossil subsidy is mostly invisible. Only $725 billion is governments directly discounting fuel. The other $6.7 trillion is "implicit" subsidy — the health damage from air pollution and the climate damage from emissions that fuel prices simply don't include. Every tank of gas is quietly co-paid by someone's lungs, someone's flooded home, someone's failed harvest. That is unconscious spending at civilizational scale · IMF, 2025 update.

The $7.3 trillion nature-negative flow is the same phenomenon on the living world: public and private money financing over-extraction, conversion and pollution — about $1.7 trillion of it straight from public treasuries. It is roughly 30× larger than everything spent protecting and restoring nature · UNEP State of Finance for Nature.

What does this buy? Exactly the curves this site documents. The money above is the financial engine behind every falling trend line in the two atlases:

GLOSSARY

The jargon on this page, in plain words.

GDP
Gross domestic product — the total value of everything the world produces in a year (~$115T). The rough size of the pool these flows draw from.
Explicit vs implicit subsidy
Explicit = government directly pays to make fuel cheaper. Implicit = the damage (health, climate) that fuel causes but its price doesn't include — society pays it anyway, just not at the pump.
Nature-negative flows
UNEP's term for public and private money financing activities that degrade nature — harmful agricultural subsidies, over-extraction, conversion of habitat.
Nature-based solutions (NbS)
Investments in nature that solve human problems — reforestation, wetland restoration, mangrove coastal defense. Currently ~$200B/yr worldwide.
Trillion
A thousand billions. One trillion dollars a year ≈ $32,000 spent every second, all year.
Capital allocation
Deciding where money goes. The thesis of this site: it is the master variable behind every environmental trend line.

Sources & method

World GDP: IMF World Economic Outlook (~$115T, 2025).

Method notes: flows come from different accounting frameworks and partially overlap (the IMF's implicit subsidies and UNEP's nature-negative flows both capture unpriced damage) — they are presented for magnitude comparison and never summed. "Unconscious/conscious" is this site's framing, not the sources'. The 3%-of-GDP redirection figure is arithmetic on the sourced numbers ($2.3T energy gap + $0.34T nature gap ≈ $2.6T ≈ 2.3% of GDP, rounded up for margin), not a published scenario.
