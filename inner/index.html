INNER TECH

Compiled by Kamber Lucas · Data current as of August 2026 · Not linked from anywhere. You clicked the sun.

Every page on this site is about decisions — where money, carbon and land go. This page is about the thing making those decisions: the mind. A wave of researchers, retreats and startups is treating inner states as trainable, measurable and fundable — and the science is further along than most people know.

START HERE

Why a money site has a consciousness page: the site argues that unconscious spending is autopilot — money moving without attention. But attention is a faculty of minds, and minds can be trained. The deepest version of "conscious cash" isn't a portfolio choice; it's the capacity to notice what you're doing while you're doing it. This page covers the emerging field — sometimes called spirit tech — trying to make that capacity scalable.

What's actually new: three things converged in the last few years. Contemplative practices that took monasteries decades are being taught in days and studied with neuroimaging at places like Harvard. Psychedelic-assisted therapy produced two positive Phase 3 trials and is on an accelerated path to FDA decision. And measurement — EEG headbands, focused ultrasound, biomarkers of deep states — is starting to make the inner world legible the way satellites made forests legible.

Same rules as the rest of the site: sources are listed at the bottom, projections are labelled as projections — and this field needs the caveats more than any other page here. The evidence base is young, the hype is real, and so are the risks. Both are covered below.

THE JHANA WAVE

The most talked-about corner of the field. Jhanas are deep absorption states — profound, stable wellbeing reached through concentration — traditionally considered to take years of practice. Jhourney, a San Francisco startup founded in 2022, claims to teach them in a week.

The numbers they report: most retreat participants reach jhana in under 40 hours of meditation; over 60% describe the retreat as the best thing that's happened to them in six months or more. Their retreats have drawn 100+ engineers from OpenAI, Anthropic and Google, and coverage from TIME. The company has raised only ~$750K — this wave is currently powered more by word of mouth than by capital.

Why it matters if it holds up: jhanas appear to be non-addictive, drug-free states of intense wellbeing. If they are reliably teachable to ordinary people in days, that is a genuinely new tool for human flourishing — one that pairs naturally with the measurement work at Harvard/MGH, where advanced states like jhana and even meditative cessation (the voluntary, temporary switching-off of consciousness) are now being captured with EEG and fMRI.

The honest read: the claims rest on self-reports from self-selected participants, and no controlled trial has yet been published. The right posture is the one this site uses everywhere: watch the data, not the vibes.

PSYCHEDELIC MEDICINE: THE CLINICAL PATH

The most regulated and best-funded corner of the field — psychedelic compounds paired with therapy, walked through the same FDA gauntlet as any drug. The last two years have been eventful in both directions.

AUG 2024
The setback: FDA rejects MDMA therapy
Lykos Therapeutics' MDMA-for-PTSD application receives a Complete Response Letter over trial-design concerns — a reminder that this path has real quality bars. A revised application was resubmitted in August 2026.
JUN 2025 · FEB 2026
Two positive Phase 3 trials for psilocybin
Compass Pathways' COMP360 hits its primary endpoint in both Phase 3 trials for treatment-resistant depression (p<0.001), with a single dose producing clinically meaningful reductions in symptoms — durability confirmed at 26 weeks (July 2026).
APR–Q4 2026 · NOW
Accelerated review
An executive order directs FDA prioritization of breakthrough-designated psychedelics; national priority vouchers go to Compass (psilocybin/TRD), Usona (psilocybin/major depression) and Transcend (an MDMA-like compound for PTSD). Compass's rolling submission completes late 2026.
LATE 2026–2027 · PROJECTED
A first approval?
If review goes well, psilocybin-assisted therapy for treatment-resistant depression could become the first FDA-approved psychedelic therapy, with launch projected for 2027. Labelled projection — regulators can still say no.

MAKING THE INVISIBLE MEASURABLE

The quiet enabling layer — the same role satellites play for the Nature Atlas. If inner states can be measured, they can be studied, trained, and taken seriously by institutions.

At the frontier: Harvard/MGH's Meditation Research Program — the first program dedicated to advanced meditation — has published neuroimaging studies of jhana and of meditative cessation, and is now testing whether transcranial focused ultrasound (tFUS) can help practitioners reach deep states faster. Early studies in experienced meditators began publishing in 2024–25.

In the lab: Templeton's $30M Accelerating Research on Consciousness program pits rival theories of consciousness against each other in pre-registered adversarial collaborations — opposing scientists agree in advance what result would prove them wrong. Foundations like Tiny Blue Dot and the Mind Science Foundation fund the same maturing field.

In your living room: consumer EEG headbands (Muse and others) put real brain signals in a $250 device. The signals are real; the evidence that feedback accelerates contemplative skill is still mixed — a fair symbol of the whole field's current state.

THE NEXT 5–20 YEARS

A qualitative outlook — clearly labelled as scenario, not prediction — for how this field could develop if the current trajectories hold.

NOW → 2030legitimacy phase
  • First approvals. Psilocybin-assisted therapy likely reaches patients through the FDA pathway; a repaired MDMA application follows. Insurance and clinical infrastructure start forming around them.
  • Replication or bust for jhana tech. The rapid-training claims meet controlled trials — the field's most falsifiable bet resolves one way or the other.
  • Measurement matures. Biomarkers for deep states firm up; adversarial collaborations narrow the space of viable consciousness theories.
2030 → 2036infrastructure phase
  • Contemplative training gets its gyms. If the science holds, structured mental training — measured, coached, insurance-adjacent — becomes a service category the way fitness did in the 1980s.
  • Therapy integration. Psychedelic clinics, meditation-based programs and conventional mental health care blur into a spectrum of dosage-controlled interventions for a ~$1T/yr problem.
  • The sector gets its capital moment — the Spirit Tech Collective's stated goal — if and only if the evidence phase above lands.
2036 → 2046integration phase
  • Attention as a trained faculty. The long bet: a meaningful share of people treat mental training the way they now treat exercise — which would quietly change consumption, politics and the "unconscious spending" this site began with.
  • Consciousness science comes of age — with mature theories, the study of wellbeing gets what climate science got from physics: a foundation.
Read this page with both eyes open: the field attracts hype faster than evidence, and it has real risks — difficult and destabilizing meditation experiences are documented (Brown University's research and the nonprofit Cheetah House exist for a reason), psychedelics carry genuine psychiatric risks outside screened, supported settings, and charismatic-teacher dynamics have burned people in every generation. Nothing here is medical advice; the interesting thing is that the serious version of this field now exists — trials, imaging, falsifiable claims — alongside the hype.

GLOSSARY

The jargon on this page, in plain words.

Spirit tech
The emerging sector applying technology and science to spiritual and contemplative development — from neurofeedback to retreat platforms to VR sanctuaries.
Jhana
A series of deep meditative absorption states marked by profound stability and wellbeing, described across 2,500 years of Buddhist practice — traditionally hard-won, now claimed teachable in days.
Cessation (nirodha samāpatti)
An advanced state in which practitioners appear to voluntarily and temporarily switch off conscious experience — now recorded in the lab with EEG and fMRI.
MBSR
Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction — the standardized 8-week program behind most of the clinical mindfulness evidence base.
Psychedelic-assisted therapy
A supervised protocol pairing a psychedelic dose with preparation and integration therapy — the form being tested in FDA trials, very different from unsupervised use.
Phase 3 trial
The final, largest stage of clinical testing before a treatment can be approved — the same bar every prescription drug must clear.
tFUS
Transcranial focused ultrasound — a non-invasive way to nudge activity in precise brain regions, now being tested as an aid to deep meditation.
Neurofeedback
Showing someone their own brain signals (usually EEG) in real time so they can learn to shift them.
Adversarial collaboration
Rival scientists co-design an experiment and agree in advance what result would prove each side wrong — science's strongest antidote to wishful thinking.
Integration
The therapy work after a psychedelic or intensive contemplative experience — turning a temporary state into a lasting change.
